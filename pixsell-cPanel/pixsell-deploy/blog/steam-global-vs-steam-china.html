Steam Global vs Steam China: What's the Difference for Developers?

China is the world's largest PC gaming market, and Steam is a key platform for reaching Chinese players. However, there are two different versions of Steam available in China: Steam Global and Steam China. Understanding the differences between these platforms is crucial for developers looking to publish their games in China.

1. Steam Global vs Steam China: The Fundamental Difference

Steam Global (International Steam)

  • The global version of Steam is available worldwide and is accessible in China (for now).
  • No government approval or ISBN license is required to publish a game.
  • Chinese players can purchase and play games on Steam Global using local payment methods (WeChat Pay, Alipay, UnionPay).
  • The full range of games is available, including those that do not comply with Chinese regulations.
  • Operates in a grey area in China: while not officially approved, it is widely used by millions of Chinese gamers.

Steam China (Official Mainland Steam)

  • Launched in 2021 as a government-approved version of Steam in partnership with Perfect World.
  • Requires an ISBN license from China's National Press and Publication Administration (NPPA).
  • Only games that pass government approval can be sold on Steam China.
  • Content is strictly regulated (e.g., restrictions on violence, political themes, and sensitive topics).
  • Fewer games available (hundreds vs. tens of thousands on Steam Global).
  • Supports local payment systems but requires real-name registration and time restrictions for underage players.

3. Regulatory Compliance: ISBN Licensing and Government Approval

Steam Global: No ISBN Required

If you publish your game on Steam Global, you do not need a Chinese ISBN or a local publisher. This makes Steam Global the easiest way to enter the Chinese market. However, because it operates in a legal grey area, it could be restricted or blocked in China in the future.

Steam China: ISBN Required

To release a game on Steam China, developers must obtain an ISBN (game licence) from China's NPPA. This requires:

  1. Partnering with a Chinese publisher (foreign developers cannot apply for an ISBN directly).
  2. Submitting the game for content review (to ensure compliance with censorship laws).
  3. Waiting for approval (this process can take 9-18 months, with no guarantee of approval).

If approved, your game is officially sanctioned in China, meaning you can market it freely on local platforms (Weibo, Bilibili, etc.). However, the process is slow, competitive, and restrictive.

4. Content Restrictions: Censorship and Compliance

Steam Global: Uncensored Content

  • Any game approved by Valve can be sold on Steam Global.
  • No modifications are required for Chinese regulations.
  • Some games banned in China (Plague Inc., Devotion) are still available via Steam Global.

Steam China: Strict Content Regulations

  • No excessive violence, skeletons, blood, or politically sensitive themes.
  • Many games require censorship changes before approval.
  • Developers must comply with China's anti-addiction laws (e.g., playtime limits for minors).

If your game contains content that might be flagged (e.g., horror, historical themes, religious elements), publishing on Steam Global may be a safer option.

5. Monetisation & Distribution: Reaching Chinese Gamers

Steam Global: Broad Distribution, No Advertising

  • Steam Global allows you to sell to Chinese players immediately, but you cannot officially market your game in China.
  • Your game might gain popularity organically through word-of-mouth, influencers, and Chinese gaming forums.
  • You receive 100% of your 70% Steam revenue share (no local publisher cut).

Steam China: Official Visibility, But Revenue Sharing

  • With an ISBN, your game can be promoted through Chinese game media, events, and paid advertising.
  • You may be able to secure higher player engagement and visibility in the long term.
  • However, revenue is typically shared with a Chinese publisher, reducing your profit margin.

If you're a smaller studio, Steam Global is often the most profitable route initially.

6. Which Route is Best for Indie Developers?

For most indie developers, publishing on Steam Global is the best first step. Here's why:

  • Instant market access: You can start selling in China immediately.
  • No ISBN or government approval required: You avoid a lengthy approval process.
  • Retain creative freedom: No content censorship needed.
  • Lower costs and better revenue share: You don't need to split earnings with a Chinese publisher.

However, if your game gains traction in China and you want long-term security and official recognition, you may consider applying for an ISBN and transitioning to Steam China.
